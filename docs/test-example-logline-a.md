## Five-sentence synopsis (Snowflake Step 2, approved 2026-11-07)

Meeting mechanism (owner approved the assistant's recommendation): **A primary,
B secondary** — the three meet on a ham radio forum (A); the signal hides a
second layer that only the three of them together can open (B).

Setting (owner chose option C on 2026-11-07): our world stays original — no
Gundam names, no UC calendar, no Zeon/Federation. The pirated anime itself is
a fictional mecha series called **"Starfall" (《星坠》)**, about a colony
falling to Earth. Fiction within fiction: the anime's plot mirrors the real
countdown, which is how the code hides in plain sight. Gundam is the
inspiration, never the setting.

1. **Setup → Chapter 1.** Dawn Lin posts his recording of a mysterious
   shortwave signal to a ham radio forum; Aoi recognises its rhythm in an old
   anime storyboard, and Leo traces its modulation to a satellite he helped
   build.
2. **Disaster 1 → Chapter 2.** The three meet in the thread and crack the first
   layer together — the signal's rhythm matches the storyboard of "Starfall," a
   pirated mecha anime about a colony falling to Earth, and the next burst
   decodes into a countdown from a runaway satellite.
3. **Midpoint (disaster 2 / reversal) → Chapter 2.** When the countdown's
   target turns out to be a satellite Leo helped build, he moves to hand the
   coordinates to the authorities — and the other two realise the signal's
   sender is not a government, but Aoi's missing brother.
4. **Disaster 3 → end of Chapter 2 / Chapter 3.** With hours left, the station
   goes dark, the team splits, and the final layer — one that only the three of
   them together can open — refuses to decode.
5. **Resolution → Chapter 3.** Aoi finds the final key in "Starfall"'s ending
   theme — her brother's handwriting in the melody — and the three broadcast
   the disarm code on the dead station's frequency, turning the countdown into
   a farewell.

### Character one-pagers (Snowflake Step 3, draft — 2026-11-07, pending owner approval)

#### Dawn Lin (林晓) — Chapter 1 POV

- **一句话：** 一位坚信短波没有过时的密码学研究生，在深夜的无线电噪音里捞到了一段不该存在的信号。
- **目标 Goal：** 证明短波理论没有过时。
- **动机 Motivation：** 他相信一段被学界 dismissed 的旧信号里藏着真实的信息。
- **冲突 Conflict：** 学界不屑他的方法；他必须信任论坛上的陌生人，而不是体制。
- **顿悟 Epiphany：** A code is not meant to be cracked. It is meant to be answered.（密码不是用来破解的，是用来回答的。）
- **一段梗概：** 林晓的弧线从“一个人听”开始，到“三个人一起播”结束。第一章，他把录音发上论坛求助，没想到回帖的两个人一个认出了节奏、一个认出了调制方式——他的理论第一次有人接住。第二章，他和两人联手解开第一层，倒计时现身；他主张继续解下去，哪怕目标指向利奥造过的卫星。第三章，电台沉寂、团队分裂，他坚持用死去电台的频率播出解除码：短波的价值不是发表论文，而是让三个人在同一时刻听见彼此。

#### Aoi Hoshino (星野葵) — Chapter 3 POV

- **一句话：** 一位画同人图的插画师，在一集旧动画的分镜里认出了失踪哥哥的笔迹。
- **目标 Goal：** 找到失踪的哥哥——《星坠》的原作者。
- **动机 Motivation：** 哥哥失踪后，《星坠》是他留下的唯一痕迹；她画他的世界，是离他最近的方式。
- **冲突 Conflict：** 她分不清哥哥留下的线索是路标还是陷阱；她一直只敢画同人，从不敢画自己的故事。
- **顿悟 Epiphany：** The puzzle was never a question. It was permission to stop searching.（谜题从来不是一个问题，而是允许她停下寻找的许可。）
- **一段梗概：** 葵的弧线是一次迟到的告别。第一章，她在林晓的录音里听出《星坠》分镜的节奏，以为只是巧合。第二章，她和利奥同时意识到信号的发送者不是政府，而是她失踪的哥哥——谜题突然有了体温。第三章，她在《星坠》的片尾曲里找到最后一把钥匙：旋律里藏着哥哥的笔迹。那一刻她明白，谜题从来不是一个问题，而是允许她停下寻找的许可；她亲手按下播出键，把倒计时变成一场告别。

#### Leo Carter (利奥·卡特) — Chapter 2 POV

- **一句话：** 一位参与过军事卫星项目的卫星通信工程师，想把坐标交给官方，却发现只有普通人能接住这段信号。
- **目标 Goal：** 赎回自己参与过的军事卫星项目。
- **动机 Motivation：** 他亲手造的东西变成了威胁，他要亲手把它收回来。
- **冲突 Conflict：** 他想把信号交给官方走体制内解决，与另两人的直觉相撞；他信任系统，系统却正是出问题的地方。
- **顿悟 Epiphany：** Redemption is not taking back what you built. It is switching it off yourself.（赎回不是把造过的东西夺回来，而是亲手把它关掉。）
- **一段梗概：** 利奥的弧线是一次信任的转移。第二章，他追踪到信号的调制方式来自自己参与建造的卫星，第一反应是上报——把坐标交给官方，让专业的人处理。可当他发现发送者是葵失踪的哥哥、而官方渠道只会把信号当作威胁封存时，他第一次站在体制外面。第三章，他放弃上报，和林晓、葵一起用死去电台的频率播出解除码：他造的卫星，最终由他亲手关掉。

## One-page synopsis (Snowflake Step 4, draft — 2026-11-07, pending owner approval)

**第 1 章（林晓视角）——求助帖。** 深夜，林晓在短波噪音里录到一段不该存在的信号：节奏里藏着结构。他把它发到业余无线电论坛求助，帖子很快有了两条奇怪的回复——一个日本插画师说，这节奏和一集旧动画《星坠》的分镜一模一样；一个美国工程师说，这调制方式来自一颗他参与建造的卫星。三个人在帖子里相遇，各自带着一个无法对别人说出口的执念：林晓要证明短波没有过时，葵要找失踪的哥哥，利奥要赎回自己造过的卫星。他们还不知道，这段信号的下一层，藏着一个倒计时。

**第 2 章（利奥视角）——倒计时与分裂。** 三人联手解开第一层：信号的节奏与盗播机甲动画《星坠》的分镜重合——那部动画讲的正是一座殖民卫星坠向地球；而下一段信号解出来，是一颗失控卫星的倒计时。倒计时的目标，竟是利奥参与建造的那颗卫星。利奥的第一反应是上报，把坐标交给官方；可就在这时，葵和林晓同时发现，信号的发送者不是什么政府，而是葵失踪的哥哥——《星坠》的原作者。信任在这一刻破裂：利奥要走官方渠道，另两人坚持自己解。只剩几小时时，电台陷入沉寂，团队分裂，而密码的最后一层——只有三人合力才能打开的一层——死活解不开。

**第 3 章（葵视角）——告别。** 葵回到《星坠》本身。她一帧一帧重看片尾曲，忽然听出来：旋律里藏着哥哥的笔迹——那是最后一把钥匙。谜题从来不是一个问题，而是允许她停下寻找的许可。三人重新聚到一起，用那座死去电台的频率播出解除码。倒计时停下的那一刻，卫星没有坠落，信号也没有消失——它变成了一场告别：三个陌生人隔着三个国家，在同一频率上，第一次真正听见了彼此。

## Character viewpoint synopses (Snowflake Step 5, optional, draft — 2026-11-07, pending owner approval)

Each retells the same story from one character's eyes, in third-person
limited. These are planning notes, not prose — the continuation agent writes
the chapters.

### Dawn Lin's viewpoint

Dawn has spent years telling himself the shortwave bands are not dead, just
unlistened-to. When he catches the signal, his first instinct is to solve it
alone — that is what a cryptographer does. The forum thread ruins that plan:
a stranger in Japan hears a storyboard in his recording, a stranger in America
recognises the modulation, and suddenly the puzzle belongs to three people.
Dawn's story is about learning that a code is not a lock to pick. When the
station goes dark and the team splits, he is the one who insists they
broadcast instead of decode — the answer is not in the signal, it is in the
reply. He presses transmit on a dead station's frequency, and for the first
time his theory is not a paper: three people hear each other at the same
moment.

### Aoi Hoshino's viewpoint

Aoi has spent two years drawing other people's worlds because her brother's
world stopped. When Dawn's recording matches a "Starfall" storyboard, she
thinks it is coincidence — then the rhythm matches again, and again, and the
coincidence starts to feel like an address. Her story is about realising the
trail was never a trail: her brother was not testing her, he was leaving her a
way to stop. The midpoint hurts the most — the sender is not a government, it
is him, and every clue she chases brings her closer to losing the search
itself. In the final chapter she stops looking for him and starts listening to
him: the ending theme holds his handwriting in the melody. She finds the key
not by solving the puzzle, but by accepting that the puzzle was permission to
say goodbye — and she is the one who broadcasts the farewell.

### Leo Carter's viewpoint

Leo built things so signals would travel further, and he has spent years
trying to outrun one thing he built. When he traces the modulation to his own
satellite, his first instinct is procedure: report it, hand over the
coordinates, let the authorities handle it. That is the whole point of a
system — someone qualified decides. His story is about discovering that the
system is the problem: the authorities would file the signal away as a threat,
and the only people who understand it are two strangers on a forum. The
midpoint forces the choice — report or stay — and he stays. In the final
chapter he does the one thing no procedure would ever authorise: he switches
off his own satellite, with his own hand, on a dead station's frequency, next
to two ordinary people. Redemption, he learns, is not taking back what you
built. It is switching it off yourself.

## Chapter arc (P0 structural roles)

- **Chapter 1 — setup + hook.** The three each intercept the same mysterious
  shortwave signal. Aoi notices the signal's rhythm matches the storyboard of
  one episode of an old anime exactly. Plants at least one open foreshadowing
  item; ends on a hook.
- **Chapter 2 — escalation.** The first layer decodes: the signal is a
  countdown from a runaway satellite. Trust breaks down; Leo almost hands the
  coordinates to the authorities. Midpoint disaster / reversal; ends on raised
  stakes.
- **Chapter 3 — payoff.** The final layer of the code hides in the anime's
  ending theme; Aoi recognises her brother's hand in it. The three use the dead
  radio station's frequency to broadcast in reverse, turning the countdown into
  a disarm code. Every open foreshadowing item is paid off; the logline is
  resolved; the protagonist's epiphany is shown.
